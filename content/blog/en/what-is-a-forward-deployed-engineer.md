A large share of software projects struggle not for technical reasons but because of communication. The business unit describes its need, an analyst turns it into a document, and a developer reads the document and writes the code. Some information is lost at every handover, and the resulting software, even when technically correct, does not fully meet the real need on the ground.

The **Forward Deployed Engineer** is a role that emerged to remove this disconnect. In this article we explain what the role is, where it comes from, how it differs from other models and what it means for organizations.

## A short definition

A Forward Deployed Engineer is an engineer who learns the client's processes on site, works directly with users, and turns what they learn into working software as the same person.

The key phrase in this definition is **the same person**. There is no handover between whoever analyzes, designs, writes the code and deploys it. The person who listens to the client's problem also writes the code that solves it.

## Where did the role come from?

The role is most closely associated with the data analytics company Palantir. While delivering software to complex corporate and government clients, Palantir found that a standard product alone was not enough. Every client's data, systems and ways of working were different. The solution was to send engineers directly to the client.

These engineers adapted the product to the client's real problem, wrote the missing pieces on site and carried what they learned back to the product team. Over time, other companies also adopted this approach as an effective way to solve complex enterprise problems.

## Why is it back on the agenda in the age of AI?

In recent years, large AI companies have also started building Forward Deployed Engineer teams. The reason is simple: having access to a powerful AI model is not the same as embedding it in an organization's real workflow.

For a model to create value in an organization, these questions must be answered:

- What data will the model access, and with what permissions?
- How will it integrate with existing systems?
- At which step will human approval be required?
- How will results be measured and audited?

The answers are not in a product document but inside the organization. The Forward Deployed Engineer is the person who finds these answers on site and turns them into a working system.

## How it differs from traditional models

| | Traditional software team | Consulting | Forward Deployed Engineer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where do they work? | In their own office | At the client | At the client, alongside the users |
| Output | Software built to a specification | A report and recommendations | Working software |
| Who do they learn the need from? | A document | Managers | The people who run the process |
| Handovers | Between analyst, designer and developer | From the report to an implementation team | Almost none |
| Feedback loop | Weeks or months | At the end of the project | Days |

A traditional software team is efficient on well-defined problems; but when the problem is unclear, the specification is wrong. Consulting defines the problem well; but its output is often a report waiting to be implemented. The Forward Deployed Engineer brings defining the problem and solving it together in one person or a small team.

## What does a Forward Deployed Engineer do day to day?

The role is quite different from desk-based engineering. A typical week includes:

- **Observing the user.** Sitting next to the employee who runs the process to see how the work is really done
- **Understanding the data.** Examining the structure, quality and limits of the data in the organization's systems
- **Rapid prototyping.** Turning the problem they see into a working first version within a few days
- **Trying it together.** Using the prototype with the user on real work to see where it falls short
- **Hardening.** Bringing the solution that works up to security, testing and maintenance standards
- **Handing over.** Training users and transferring knowledge to the organization's team

The most valuable part of this loop is speed. A misunderstood need is caught in the first week's prototype, not in software delivered months later.

## What skills does the role require?

A Forward Deployed Engineer must have all the technical skills of a good software engineer. What sets the role apart are the qualities needed alongside them:

- **Speaking the language of the business.** Talking to an HR specialist, a lawyer or a finance manager in their own terms
- **Working with ambiguity.** Defining the problem from observation without waiting for a clear specification
- **Prioritization.** Doing first not every request, but the one that creates the most value
- **Ownership.** Feeling responsible not for writing the code but for genuinely making the user's work easier
- **Corporate discipline.** Building security, regulatory and audit requirements into the design from the start

## What does it mean for organizations?

The Forward Deployed approach brings organizations three concrete benefits.

**The right problem gets solved.** Because the need is learned on site from the people who run the process, the software fits the real workflow. Nobody hears "this is not what we asked for" after delivery.

**Value shows up sooner.** Instead of an analysis and development cycle lasting months, the first working version reaches users within weeks. Whether the investment works becomes clear early.

**Knowledge stays in the organization.** The engineer does not just deliver software; they train the organization's team, document the process and hand over the source code. The organization does not become dependent on the vendor.

## When is it the right model?

This approach is not necessary for every project. It is especially valuable when:

- The process is complex, full of exceptions and not fully defined in writing
- Several departments and systems are involved
- Users find it hard to describe what they want, but recognize it immediately when they see it
- It is not yet clear how a new technology such as AI will fit into the organization's workflow

On the other hand, for work with clear and standard requirements, a traditional development model or a ready-made product is often the better fit.

## Conclusion

> A Forward Deployed Engineer learns the client's processes on site and turns them directly into working software. By removing the handover between analysis and development, the role makes sure the right problem is solved in less time.
>
> The role matters again in the age of AI because access to technology has become easy, while embedding it in an organization's real work is still done on the ground, together with the user.
