In most organizations, the HR team runs dozens of training sessions and events every year: onboarding, mandatory training, leadership programmes, company gatherings. Most of these are still run with the same tools: an Excel file, an e-mail inbox and plenty of reminders.

This method works, and that is exactly where the problem starts. Because it works, nobody questions its cost. Yet the cost of tracking training attendance by hand can add up to the full-time work of one or two employees by the end of the year. In this article we explain where this cost hides, how to calculate it for your own organization and what a good training tracking system should include.

## Behind the scenes of a training session

From the participant's point of view, a training session is an invitation e-mail and a few hours in a session. From the HR team's point of view, each of the following steps is a separate task:

1. **Planning.** Collecting training needs and setting the date, trainer and capacity.
2. **Announcement and invitation.** Building the list of target employees and sending invitations.
3. **Registration and capacity.** Collecting replies and managing a waiting list once capacity is full.
4. **Reminders and changes.** Last-minute cancellations, swaps and date changes.
5. **Attendance.** Recording who attended on the day.
6. **Certificates.** Preparing and sending certificates to attendees.
7. **Evaluation.** Sending satisfaction surveys and collecting the results.
8. **Reporting.** Training hours per employee, completion of mandatory training, reports for management and audit.

In a team working with Excel and e-mail, almost all of these steps are done by hand, and many are repeated more than once.

## Where does the hidden cost hide?

In a manual training process, time is lost in losses that look small one by one:

| Loss | How it happens |
|---|---|
| Re-entering data | The same attendee details are typed separately into the invitation list, the registration sheet, the attendance list and the certificate |
| Matching | Registrations received by e-mail are matched by hand with the HR system for employee number and department |
| Handling changes | Every cancellation or date change is updated separately in every list |
| Preparing certificates | Certificates are prepared one by one, names are checked, and they are sent one by one |
| Compiling reports | Data from different files is merged for the year-end report and inconsistencies are cleaned up |
| Dependence on one person | Only the person running the process knows which file is current; when they are on leave, the process slows down |

On top of this comes the cost of errors. An invitation sent to the wrong person, a certificate with a wrong name or a mandatory training that slips through the cracks carries reputational and compliance risk beyond the time it takes to fix.

## Calculate your own cost

You do not need a complex analysis to see this cost. One month of measurement is enough to build the annual picture.

**1. Write down the steps and their durations.** For each of the eight steps above, note the average time spent on a single training session.

**2. Set the annual volume.** How many training sessions and events are held per year, with how many attendees on average?

**3. Calculate the hourly cost.** Divide the total employer cost of the people running the process (salary, benefits, social security contributions) by their annual working hours.

**4. Apply the formula.**

**Annual operating cost** = total time per training session (hours) × training sessions per year × hourly cost

For steps that depend on the number of attendees (such as matching and certificates), calculating the time per attendee and multiplying by the number of attendees gives a more accurate result.

## What a training tracking system should include

Whether you are evaluating a ready-made product or planning a custom system, you can use the list below as a checklist:

- **Employee data from a single source.** Name, employee number, department and manager should not be typed in by hand but come from the HR system or the corporate directory.
- **Capacity and waiting list.** When capacity is full, registrations should go to a waiting list automatically, and a cancellation should free a place for the next person.
- **Automatic invitations and reminders.** Invitations, calendar entries and reminders should be sent from the system.
- **Easy attendance.** Attendance on the day should be quick to record.
- **Automatic certificates.** Certificates should be generated from a template and sent automatically to everyone whose attendance is confirmed.
- **Mandatory training tracking.** It should be possible to see who has not completed which mandatory training and to send reminders.
- **Evaluation survey.** The post-training survey should be sent automatically and its results linked to the session.
- **Ready-made reports.** Training hours per employee, attendance by department and completion rates should be available in one click.
- **Permissions and personal data security.** Who can see which data should be set by role, and personal data should be processed and stored in line with the law.

## Off-the-shelf training software or a custom system?

The market offers many ready-made learning management systems designed to manage online training content. If most of your training consists of videos and online modules, these products are often a good start.

A custom system comes to the fore when:

- A significant share of training is held in person and the organizational workload is high,
- Events and training are run by the same team, with the same process,
- The system needs to work with the organization's intranet, HR system and corporate directory,
- A ready-made product has been bought, but Excel files and e-mail threads still revolve around it.

We cover the criteria to weigh when comparing the two options in our [off-the-shelf or custom software guide](../build-vs-buy-custom-software/).

## Where to start?

The first step is not choosing software but measuring. Over the next month, note the time spent on each of the eight steps above for every training session held. At the end of the month you will have both a realistic estimate of the annual cost and a list of the steps where most time is lost. That list often shows on its own which solution suits you.

## Conclusion

> Running training and events with Excel and e-mail is a method whose cost goes unnoticed because it works. The cost hides in re-entering data, matching, preparing certificates and compiling reports.
>
> Once you measure these steps, the annual cost of training attendance tracking is often large enough to cover a software investment within a year.
